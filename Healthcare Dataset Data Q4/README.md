1. High Inconclusive Diagnostic RateInsight: 18,198 test results (33.11% of all admissions) were returned as "Inconclusive," matching the volume of Abnormal (33.54%) and Normal (33.35%) results.   
Evidence: Analysis of diagnostic result distribution shows 18,198 inconclusive outcomes out of 54,966 cleaned patient records.  
Why Selected: It reveals potential operational bottlenecks in laboratory processing or equipment calibration, impacting diagnostic velocity.   
Business Impact: Drives up secondary testing costs, delays definitive treatment pathways, and increases patient overall length of stay.   
Recommendation: Establish strict lab re-testing protocols, recalibrate diagnostic machinery, and implement mandatory peer-reviews for inconclusive results.  
2. Flat Billed Rates Across Complex vs. Chronic ConditionsInsight: Mean billing per admission remains virtually identical across all conditions: Cancer averages $25,154.73 while Obesity averages $25,806.63.   
Evidence: Billed charges across six major medical conditions show minimal variance, ranging between $25,154 and $25,806.   
Why Selected: Highlights a lack of acuity-adjusted or tiered pricing, signaling potential under-billing on resource-heavy treatments.   
Business Impact: Hospital risks under-recovering costs on complex oncological cases while over-charging low-acuity treatments, causing margin compression.   
Recommendation: Conduct an activity-based cost accounting (ABC) audit to transition from flat pricing to severity/acuity-adjusted billing models.   
3. Uniform Length of Stay (LOS) Across Admission TypesInsight: Mean Length of Stay is flat across Emergency (15.58 days), Elective (15.51 days), and Urgent (15.40 days) admissions.   Evidence: Date duration calculations (Discharge Date minus Date of Admission) show average stay duration does not significantly vary by admission type.   
Why Selected: Emergency admissions typically require longer stabilization than scheduled elective cases; equal stay indicates discharge inefficiencies.   
Business Impact: Inefficient bed turnover blocks emergency intake channels and incurs unnecessary room occupancy costs.   
Recommendation: Implement fast-track discharge planning units specifically for elective cases and establish emergency bed turnover targets.   
4. Parallel Reimbursements Across Insurance PayersInsight: Average billing per case ranges within a tight window of $25,415.44 (UnitedHealthcare) to $25,630.16 (Medicare) across all 5 major payers.   
Evidence: Mean billing breakdown across Medicare, Blue Cross, Aetna, Cigna, and UnitedHealthcare shows near-identical average billing amounts.   
Why Selected: Demonstrates an absence of negotiated rate differentials or tiered payer structures across commercial vs. public programs.   
Business Impact: Leaves money on the table due to unutilized bargaining leverage with high-volume commercial insurers.  
Recommendation: Renegotiate payer contracts to establish tiered reimbursement rates tied to overall volume commitments and specialty care.   
5. Equal Gender & Demographic DistributionInsight: Patient volume is split equally between Males (27,496 admissions) and Females (27,470 admissions) across all chronic conditions.   Evidence: Categorical counts show a near 50/50 breakdown in admission volume by gender.   Why Selected: Resource allocation and clinical outreach must remain multi-specialty rather than leaning towards gender-specific health programs.   
Business Impact: Broad operational focus prevents over-investing in specialized single-gender facilities while optimizing general bed utilization.   
Recommendation: Maintain balanced capacity allocation across general wards and tailor preventive care marketing equally across demographics.
